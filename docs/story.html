Let's be honest

You've heard this promise before.
So why did it stall?

🧗

Citizen development hit a wall: the tool itself

"Anyone can build" turned into "anyone who learns a designer, Power Fx formulas, and delegation limits." Model-driven apps meant tables, forms, views, and solutions. Most knowledge workers took a look, felt intimidated, and went back to Excel. "Citizen developer" ended up meaning a small group of power users.

🌊

Governance hit a wall: Default overload

Every licensed user could build in the Default environment, so they did. Apps and flows piled up in one shared space with no ownership plan, no lifecycle, and no line between experiments and business-critical tools. Admins spent their time cleaning up after the fact.

Two walls. One kept most people out. The other let everything in without a plan.

So what's actually different?

The promise isn't new. The barriers finally are.

🏛️ Environment governance

Every app gets this. It works the same as it always has.

  • Connectors & connectionsThe same catalog of 1,300+, the same connection model
  • Data policies (DLP)Tenant and environment rules decide what an app may connect to
  • Admin oversightApps are registered to an environment, so they're visible and governed there

🗄️ Dataverse

Optional. It's one way to govern data, used only when the use case needs it.

  • Tables, relationships & rolesSecured storage for data the app owns
  • SolutionsManaged solutions carry that schema between environments

Many apps never touch Dataverse. They work with data where it already lives, like SharePoint, SQL, Excel, or any connector, and that system's own permissions protect it.
